Government Tech

May 2026

By Dr. Abiola Oyebanjo

What makes government technology actually work in Nigeria?

Nigeria has no shortage of government technology projects. Portals are launched, dashboards are built, apps are deployed. Most of them are not being used twelve months later. The question is not why Nigeria lacks technology — it is why the technology that exists does not get used.

The answer, consistently, comes down to three things: the tool was designed without the people who would use it; it required skills or infrastructure the institution did not have; and when the project ended, there was no one left to maintain it. These are not technical failures. They are design and implementation failures.

Technology that works in Nigerian government is almost always simple, familiar, and co-designed with frontline workers from the start. It runs on tools people already know. It has internal champions who can fix it when things go wrong. And it is designed to survive the end of the project that created it.

Data Systems

May 2026

By Dr. Nneka Osadolor

The data problem inside Nigerian government agencies

Walk into almost any Nigerian government office and you will find data being collected. Logbooks, attendance registers, patient cards, incident forms. The data is there. The problem is what happens to it next — which is usually nothing.

Data collected by frontline workers rarely reaches the people who make decisions. It sits in forms and notebooks because there is no system for aggregating it, no process for analysing it, and often no one whose job it is to do so. The result is that managers make decisions based on intuition or tradition rather than evidence — not because they want to, but because the evidence they need is locked in a format they cannot use.

Fixing this does not require sophisticated technology. It requires closing the gap between data collection and data use — creating simple pathways from the notebook to the dashboard, and ensuring someone reviews the dashboard and acts on what it shows.

Co-Design

April 2026

By Akintoye Oyekunle

Why we start with frontline workers, not leadership

When organisations bring in technology consultants, meetings typically start at the top. The Director-General explains the problem. Senior managers describe what they need. A solution is designed, presented back to leadership, approved, and handed to frontline workers to use. It does not work.

The reason is simple: the people who know the real problem are the people doing the work, not the people managing it. A police commander knows patrols are inefficient. The officer on the ground knows exactly why — and it is rarely the reason the commander thinks it is. A principal knows attendance is poor. The class teacher knows which students are at risk and why they are drifting.

We start at the bottom because that is where the real problem lives, and because the people who understand the problem are the people who will use the tool. If they shaped it, they will use it. If it was handed to them, they will not.

Design Philosophy

April 2026

By Dr. Abiola Oyebanjo

Simple tools beat complex software: our design philosophy

Every government technology project we have seen fail has had the same problem: the solution was too complex for the context it was deployed in. A system requiring stable internet to function deployed in an area with intermittent connectivity. A dashboard requiring dedicated IT support in an institution with no IT department. Software with a steep learning curve given to workers who were already stretched thin.

Our rule is simple: if a government worker cannot learn to use the tool in two hours and maintain it themselves, it is the wrong tool. This is not a compromise. It is a design principle. The best solution is the one that actually gets used, not the most technically sophisticated one.

In practice, this means Google Sheets for data entry. Power BI for visualisation. WhatsApp for check-ins. These are tools officers, teachers, and health workers already have on their phones. The infrastructure is already there. We just connect it to something useful.
