No one should re-prove what the state already knows

November 2026  ·  Abiola Oyebanjo

A citizen should not have to prove, again and again, something one arm of government already knows. Today they often must. That is a design problem, and it can be fixed.

Picture a farmer who needs to confirm she is who she says she is to receive support. One agency already holds that fact. The agency in front of her cannot see it, so she is sent away to fetch a letter, a stamp, a printout, and to come back. The information existed the whole time. It just could not travel the short distance between two offices.

Multiply that by millions of interactions and you get the quiet tax that poor coordination places on people, most heavily on those with the least time, money and documents to spare. They are the ones who cannot easily make the second trip.

The fix is not a bigger database that holds everything. It is a way for one agency to ask another a single, approved question and get a trusted answer back.

That is the second part of our work. It lets agencies confirm a fact for one another without pooling or handing over records. A question goes in. A verified answer comes out. The underlying data never leaves the agency that holds it.

We are not detailing the machinery in public yet. The point for now is the outcome we are building toward. The state should carry the burden of remembering what it knows, so that citizens do not have to carry it for them.
