Why we chose crime and violence as our first focus area

May 2026  ·  Abiola Oyebanjo

When we decided to focus the 1864 Institute's first two years on crime, violence, and public safety, the decision was not arbitrary. It came from a specific observation about where the gap between data and decision-making is most consequential, most tractable, and least served by existing organisations.

The gap is large and well-documented

Nigeria's security challenges are significant, widely discussed, and the subject of considerable policy attention. What is less discussed is how much of the institutional response to crime is made without adequate data. Patrol decisions are made on habit. Incident data is collected but never aggregated. Commanders work from memory, not from evidence. This is not because the people involved do not care. It is because the systems that would give them better information do not exist or are not used.

The problem is specific and solvable

We chose crime and violence partly because the problem is specific enough to be solvable at the institutional level. A police division has data. It has a commander who makes decisions. The gap between those two things is a data flow problem, not a political or budgetary one. That makes it a problem we can actually work on, with real people, in a real institution, in a reasonable timeframe.

The evidence gap matters for citizens who have no alternative

Poor Nigerians cannot purchase private security when public safety fails them. They cannot pay for private policing when the institutions that are supposed to protect them are operating blind. The case for improving how security institutions use data is therefore not just a governance argument. It is an equity argument. When public safety institutions work better, the people who benefit most are those who depend on them entirely.

We are not naive about the scale of what we are taking on. We are starting small, with one institution, focused on a specific problem. But if we can demonstrate in one place that better data tools change how security decisions are made, that is a replicable model. And replicable models are what the 1864 Institute exists to build.
